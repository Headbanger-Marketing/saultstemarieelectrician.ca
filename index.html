Picture a basement left unfinished, with the panel and the mechanical equipment in it. While its joists stay open, cable routes can be easier to plan, but that room may be drywalled later, so the plan should record what stays reachable.

What paperwork should I keep after each stage of the work?

Each phase should leave a written scope, a note of what was installed and where, labels on any new circuits and the ESA notification details. Keep them together so the next contractor does not have to guess what came before.

If funds are tight, which job should be done first?

Put safety concerns and anything that blocks later work ahead of convenience upgrades. Ask the contractor to rank the items and explain why, so the order reflects the condition of the installation and not only the price of each piece.

What makes quotes for separate stages hard to compare fairly?

Line the quotes up by scope, not by total. Check that every one names the same circuits, the same finishing work and the same notification, and ask whether any phase depends on work another quote leaves out.

Will walls going up around the panel limit later access?

Only a contractor can judge that. Ask whether the current spot keeps clear working space and easy access once walls or a ceiling go up, and get the answer written into the scope for the first phase.

Who confirms the phases and the price after I send my plan?

Phases and price would come separately from a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor. Treat this site as reading matter; it reviews your enquiry without arranging visits, setting prices or doing electrical work. Before agreeing to any phase, have that provider confirm its licence and identity, and check the licence through ESA. Settle scope and timing with that provider.
